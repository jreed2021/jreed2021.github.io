HEALTHCARE ANALYTICS CASE STUDY

CMS Medical Care Ratings & Enrollee Experience

Are stronger CMS Medical Care ratings associated with better enrollee-reported health plan experiences? A CMS Marketplace analysis across 308 reporting units.

PythonPandasMySQLTableauHealthcare Analytics
View GitHub RepositoryRead Published AnalysisView Tableau Dashboard

PROJECT OVERVIEW

The Research Question

CMS Marketplace quality reporting includes Medical Care performance and enrollee-reported health plan experience. I investigated whether higher Medical Care star ratings consistently corresponded to higher adjusted experience ratings.

Analytical goalExamine the relationship between two distinct dimensions of plan performance without interpreting correlation as causation.

DATASET

Cleaning and Validation

331QRS reporting units matched to Enrollee Survey records
23Excluded for missing numeric Medical Care values
308Final unique reporting units

I cleaned CMS public-use data in Python, handled reporting codes such as CSR-I, created a reporting-unit join key, checked overlap, and validated a one-to-one merge. The 308-row analytical sample was then reconciled in MySQL.

Data quality lessonAn initial MySQL import loaded only 298 rows because CMS-coded text values had been assigned numeric types. After correcting the import schema, the total row count and distinct reporting-unit count both equaled 308.

FINDING 01

Higher Star Ratings Did Not Consistently Correspond to Better Experience

Average adjusted enrollee experience scores varied across Medical Care star ratings rather than increasing steadily.

InterpretationPlans with higher Medical Care ratings did not necessarily receive the highest average adjusted enrollee experience scores in this dataset.

FINDING 02

Weak Negative Correlations

−0.132Pearson correlation
−0.159Spearman correlation
−0.151Experience-score skewness

Both correlation measures indicated weak negative associations between Medical Care scores and adjusted health plan ratings. This does not mean higher Medical Care performance caused worse enrollee experience; the measures describe different aspects of plan performance.

FINDING 03

Investigating Differences Between Performance and Experience

Several reporting units with 4- or 5-star Medical Care ratings showed comparatively large gaps between Medical Care scores and enrollee experience scores. I used gap calculations to identify units worth closer review, not as a standard that scores should be identical.

METHODOLOGY

Python → MySQL → Tableau

Python: intake, cleaning and exploratory analysis

Reviewed data types, duplicates and missingness; converted relevant fields to numeric values; created REPORTING_UNIT_ID; validated the merge; and calculated descriptive statistics, skewness and correlations.

MySQL: verification and comparisons

Reconciled row counts and unique reporting units, summarized experience by Medical Care rating, reviewed plan types and state-level averages using a minimum sample-size rule, and calculated score differences.

Tableau: communication

Built views of average experience by Medical Care rating, Medical Care score versus experience with a trend line, and the largest gaps among 4- and 5-star reporting units. The reconciled CSV served as the Tableau Public data source.

CONCLUSION

Medical Care and Enrollee Experience Capture Different Dimensions

The results did not show a consistent increase in enrollee-reported experience as Medical Care ratings rose. Small negative correlations and substantial differences for some highly rated reporting units suggest that clinical performance metrics and member experience should be evaluated separately.

Main takeaway

The analysis is descriptive and observational. Correlation does not establish a causal relationship, and the score-gap measure is a screening aid rather than a performance target.
